# The formula: how each channel's Shorts are built

This is the distilled result of the research in [`README.md`](README.md) and [`research/`](research/).
It is a working hypothesis built from what the leaders in each niche do and from what YouTube
says about how it ranks Shorts. **Our own analytics settle it**: see §5 for how to test it.

## 1. The shared engine (all three channels)

The Shorts feed shows each new Short to a small test audience, mostly non-subscribers, often within
the first hour. Their reactions decide whether it is shown to more people. The reactions that count:
whether they **watch or swipe away**, **how much of it they watch, and whether they replay it**,
likes, shares and comments, and **satisfaction surveys**. YouTube publishes the signals, not their
weights. Since 31 Mar 2025 every replay counts as a public view; pay and ranking use "engaged views".

So every Short has to win four moments:

| Moment | What decides it | Our rule |
|---|---|---|
| **0–2 s: watch or swipe** | The feed autoplays with no thumbnail, so frame 0 *is* the thumbnail | Frame 0 already shows the problem in motion. The voice starts on frame 0. The hook is on screen as text and matches the spoken line. No logo, title card, greeting or calm establishing shot |
| **2 s → the end: keep watching** | % viewed falls with length unless something new keeps arriving | A new beat every **3–6 s**. The payoff comes **before** the end. Burned-in captions. Every spoken noun is shown |
| **The end: replay** | Loops add views and push % viewed above 100% | The last frame matches the first; the last line flows back into the first. No outro, no "subscribe". Music has no final cadence |
| **After: comment, share, satisfaction** | Comments come from recognition, disagreement and choices, not from being asked | One honest, specific question tied to the story, spoken or shown, and the same question pinned. Accurate facts. No bait that cheats (a promised payoff that never comes hurts satisfaction) |

**Channel-level rules** (the "inauthentic content" policy, July 2025, and the 2026 crackdown on "AI slop"):
- Each episode has its own story, script and set dressing. A series is fine; a clone with one word swapped is not.
- Keep the three channels clearly distinct in look and topic.
- Original characters, real jokes, checked facts and visible craft protect us. So do sources in the description.
- Stylised animation with a generic TTS narrator needs no "altered or synthetic content" label. A realistic human or a cloned real voice would.
- **Made for Kids**: answer honestly per channel. "Made for Kids" turns off comments and the notification bell. If a channel is not aimed at children, keep its framing general-audience: no kid-bait emoji strings, no "for kids" in the metadata.

**Cadence**: 3–5 Shorts a week per channel, on a fixed schedule. Per-Short results are best at about 2–4 a week (Metricool's guidance); total views grow with volume. Publish late afternoon or evening US time (Thu–Sat, 4–7 pm ET, per Buffer's study), then switch to Studio's "when your viewers are on YouTube" once the data exists.

**Packaging**:
- **Title:** plain, searchable, the key noun up front, one emoji at most, ≤ 60 characters.
- **Hashtags:** 2–4 in the description, most relevant first; #Shorts is optional.
- **Cover:** custom Shorts covers now reach all creators (reported 25 Nov 2026). They show on the channel grid, in search and on Home shelves, never in the swipe feed. Design one frame per Short that works as both.
- **Links:** on every Short, set a **related-video link** to the next episode or the best performer. Put recurring formats into **Shorts series** once that feature reaches us.
- **Crossovers:** credit the three channels as co-creators on crossover Shorts (the Collaborations feature allows up to 5–10 co-creators).

## 2. Pocket City

**One-line formula:** *a visible crisis in a tiny town, a clock you can read, three escalating beats, a satisfying save, and the town back where it started.*

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–2.0 s | **Crisis already in frame.** VO: "You have 47 seconds." The countdown is in the world (harbour clock, signal board) | The hero (or the two routes, or the two tugs) is the one saturated thing in the sharp band |
| 2–8 s | **Stakes and rule:** what happens at zero; a route map inset appears | Optional choice beat: "Tunnel or bridge? Pick one." |
| 8–26 s | **Three escalating problems**, each bigger: one car → a bus → the bridge | Time-lapse stutter locked to the beat; each beat ends on a near-miss |
| 26–33 s | **The save**, at ×1 REAL TIME as the clock hits single digits | The style's speed readout carries the drama |
| 33–38 s | **Payoff + loop line**: a one-line true fact where it fits ("That's a phantom jam: nobody caused it."), then the line that leads into the opening | The camera rises back to the opening aerial; the last frame matches frame 0 |

- **Length:** 30–40 s.
- **Title shapes:**
  - `Can 1 Tow Truck Clear 200 Cars?`
  - `What If Every Traffic Light Turned Green?`
  - `The Drawbridge Won't Close`
  - `Tunnel or Bridge? 40 Seconds`
  - Add the series number in the description and the series playlist, not the title.
- **Series:** Race the Clock · One Rule Changed · Will It Survive? · Pick a Side.
- **Comment hook:** the choice ("Did you pick tunnel?") or "What would you have done?".
- **Rewatch hook:** the same yellow cat hidden on a roof in every film.
- **Cover:** the crisis at the moment of peak tension, with the clock visible; tilt-shift blur leaves room for a 2–3 word caption.
- **Avoid:** mascot faces, cartoon kid-music, emoji-heavy titles (this keeps the channel general-audience). Avoid long calm openings.

## 3. Pixel Life

**One-line formula:** *the same little pixel hero, a new job, the customer's absurd line in frame 1, three disasters as game events, "YOU'RE FIRED", and a last line that loops into the first.*

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–2.0 s | **Cold open on the funniest moment**: the customer's line in a dialogue box ("I'd like to return this." A cake with one slice missing), or the disaster in mid-air | Text and voice on frame 0; the hero is in the one colour no set uses |
| 2–5 s | **"You are a returns desk clerk."** A quest banner: `QUEST: survive 26 December` | The job named in the first spoken words |
| 5–35 s | **Three disasters, each bigger**, every 5–7 s; HUD gags (patience bar draining, XP, "!" bubbles); The Customer / The Manager / The Cat recur | Every gag reads with the sound off |
| 35–40 s | **The fall:** "GAME OVER: YOU'RE FIRED" card, or the twist that saves the day | Sting on the beat |
| 40–45 s | **Echo line → loop**: the last words flow into "You are a …" | The last frame matches frame 0 (same desk, same bell) |

- **Length:** 40–45 s by default; 55–61 s only for flagship episodes with enough gags.
- **Title shapes:**
  - `You Are a Hotel Receptionist`, plus a twist in the description;
  - `POV: Day 1 as a Barista`;
  - `Every Returns Desk on December 26`.
  - Put the job in the first three words. The episode counter `Job #4` goes in the description and on screen.
- **Recurring:** the hero (fixed sprite), The Customer, The Manager, The Cat. Crossovers between jobs (the same hotel).
- **Comment hook:** "Receptionists: what's the worst you've had?" Pin "Which job should he get fired from next?" and make the winner.
- **Seasons:** retail in Nov–Dec, Mall Santa in Dec, ski lift in Jan–Feb, tax season in Mar–Apr, lifeguard in Jun–Aug, haunted house in Oct.
- **Cover:** a tight crop of the sprite plus the disaster, and a 2–4 word pixel-font caption ("DAY 1. FIRED?").

## 4. Some Crazy Thingzz

**One-line formula:** *the object huge in frame 1 with the question, a confidently wrong character acts out an absurd theory (stamped WRONG), the "Actually…" sting, the true answer shown in one clear demo, and a final line that asks the question again.*

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.5 s | **The object in close-up**, the odd detail highlighted (circle or glow). VO asks the question; it is on screen as text | The title is the same question |
| 1.5–10 s | **The wrong theory**, acted out by the confidently wrong character (fixed name and role) | Absurd and visual; a big "❌ WRONG" stamp and buzzer at its end, so no viewer takes it as fact |
| ~10 s | **"Actually…"**: the same sting every episode; the character who knows steps in | A recognisable moment |
| 10–25 s | **The true answer, shown**: one cut-away or side-by-side demo (round pencil rolls, hexagonal one stops) | ≤ 2 sentences of mechanism; every noun on screen |
| 25–30 s | **Callback gag + loop line**: the wrong character tries a new theory on the next object, or asks the same question again | Hard cut, no outro |

- **Length:** 25–35 s. At about 2.7 words a second that is about 70–95 words of script.
- **Titles:** the searched question itself, `Why Do Pencils Have Six Sides?`, with at most one emoji.
- **Recurring:** two named characters with fixed roles (keep their existing names if they have them): the **confidently wrong one**, whose theories get wilder each episode, and **the one who knows**. Put the channel logo inside every set (a mug, a poster, a cereal box). Use the same TTS voice settings and the same reveal sting.
- **Trust:** every answer is checked against two reputable sources, and a source line goes in the description. Myths get their own "Myth Busted" episodes. Corrections in the comments get a heart and, when they're right, a follow-up Short.
- **Go one layer deeper:** the top ~30 "hidden purpose" facts are widely known. Add the second layer (pen cap hole → pen barrel hole) or a twist ("…and it's the law").
- **Series:** Holes in Things · Sports Balls · Road Stuff · Kitchen Secrets · Myth Busted.
- **Comment hook:** "What did YOU think it was for?" Pin "Which object next?"
- **Cover:** the object huge, the detail circled, the wrong character's shocked face.

## 5. How to prove the formula (test plan)

1. **Track weekly per channel** in Studio: "chose to view" % (viewed vs swiped away), average % viewed (and whether it goes above 100%), engaged views, subscribers per 1,000 engaged views, comments per 1,000 views. Ignore raw views: since 2025 they count swipe-throughs too.
2. **Change one thing at a time** and compare 3–5 Shorts before against 3–5 after:
   - test 1: cold open on the crisis/customer line vs the current opening (watch "chose to view");
   - test 2: length, 40 s vs 55 s (watch % viewed and engaged views);
   - test 3: recurring hero / named characters on vs off (watch subscribers per 1,000 views);
   - test 4: choice question vs no question (watch comments per 1,000).
3. When YouTube's "test up to 3 versions" feature arrives (announced for 2027), render 2–3 cold opens per Short. Code-made films make this nearly free.
4. **Rules of thumb** for reading the numbers (creator practice, not official): "chose to view" ≥ 60–70% is healthy and under 50% means the hook failed; % viewed ≥ 80% under 35 s and ≥ 60–70% above 60 s; > 100% means people are replaying it.

## 6. Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Frame 0: the problem in motion, the hook text on screen, the voice starting at once
- [ ] The key noun or job in the first spoken sentence and in the title
- [ ] A new beat every 3–6 s; the payoff before the end
- [ ] Burned-in captions; readable with the sound off
- [ ] Last frame = first frame; the last line flows into the first; no outro
- [ ] One specific question, spoken or on screen, and the same one pinned
- [ ] One hidden rewatch detail
- [ ] Facts checked against two sources, listed in the description (Some Crazy Thingzz)
- [ ] Cover frame chosen or designed; 2–4 hashtags; related-video link set; added to its series
- [ ] Made for Kids answered honestly; no real brands, places or people
- [ ] Not a near-copy of the previous episode
