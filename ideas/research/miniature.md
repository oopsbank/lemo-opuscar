# Pocket City: research on adjacent niches (miniature, traffic and sim, chain reaction, vehicles, second-person "what if")

Researched 2026-11-26 with about 33 web searches and fetches. **How reliable the numbers are:** subscriber and view counts come from third-party trackers (vidIQ, Social Blade, Viewstats, Trari, HypeAuditor, Playboard, youtubers.me), and these often disagree with each other, sometimes by a factor of 2 (Miniatur Wunderland: 886K on youtubers.me against 377K on HypeAuditor). Each figure below carries its source and the date it was reported. Figures from vendor blogs (AI-video tools) are marked **[vendor, unverified]**. I could not open YouTube Studio data or per-Short retention data. The web search tools could not find hook descriptions for individual Shorts, so where I describe a hook from titles or descriptions I say so.

## 1. The leading channels and what they do

### A. 3D-animated "how / what happens if" Shorts: the model to beat
**Zack D. Films** (@zackdfilms)
- **Scale:** about 29.8M subscribers and about 83.7B views (Trari, 22 Nov 2026: https://trari.com/en/c/zackdfilms/; Viewstats: https://www.viewstats.com/@zackdfilms/channelytics). About +100K subscribers a week. The top Short, "How Stitches Work", has 250M+ views (https://zackdfilms.fandom.com/wiki/Zack_D._Films).
- **Format:** fully 3D-animated micro-documentaries under 60 s, with fast voice-over. The channel moved to near-100% CGI by the end of 2023.
- **Hook:** always visual, in the first 1 to 3 s: a shocking close-up with no intro and no call to action.
- **Loop:** the narrative returns to its opening, so the Short loops seamlessly. Videos end abruptly.
- **Cadence:** reported as 4 to 5 Shorts a day, about 150 a month.
- **Comments:** gross or controversial visuals and "what if" scenarios start debates in the comments.

Sources for the format details: https://ivideonow.com/blog/why-zack-d-films-dominates-youtube-shorts-with-unsettling-3d-animation-content-en **[vendor, unverified]**, https://cool3dworld.com/Zack-D-Films.

- **Titles:** "How X works", "What happens if you…", "Why you should never…".
- **Audience:** broad. Many children watch, and child-safety advocates have criticised the gross-out content (same vendor source).
- **Lesson for us:** this is the closest proven format to ours (CGI, narration, 30 to 60 s, the "what happens if" frame). The differences: their hook is a body or object in peril, the payoff is a fact, and the end loops back to the opening.

### B. Physics-sim vehicle destruction (BeamNG.drive)
**BeamngShorts** (@beamngshorts): about 7.2M subscribers and 5B+ views, active through Oct 2026 (Social Blade: https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/beamngshorts).
- **mrgot beamng drive crashes:** about 1.44M subscribers and 1.21B views from only 197 videos, data Mar 2025 (https://socialblade.com/youtube/channel/UCSi5HWpN9ZWbl9Coi1qcTng).
- **BeamshortS:** 758K subscribers and 1.06B views from 1,362 videos, late Oct 2026 (vidIQ: https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@beamshorts/).
- **BeamNG Nation:** its top video, trains into a giant pit, had 204M views by Aug 2022 (https://wiki.sportskeeda.com/youtube/who-is-beamng-nation).
- **Title pattern:** "[Vehicle] vs [Obstacle]" plus emoji, e.g. "Tanker Truck Tow Pickup vs Upside Down Speed Bumps! 🚛💥 | BeamNG.drive #Shorts". Kid-meme crossovers appear too ("Alphabet Lore vs Cars 😂").
- **Recurring series:** numbered episodes ("Trains vs Speed Bumps #65", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Ph2mlcp2Co). Variables: speed, vehicle size, obstacle type.
- **Audience:** heavily skewed to kids and young teens. The meme crossovers point that way, but this is my inference.
- **Structure:** a single escalating test with no narration. The same obstacle is hit by bigger and bigger vehicles, or at faster and faster speeds.
- **Lesson:** "X vs Y" escalation ladders and numbered series. Variety comes from swapping one variable.

### C. Physical miniature disaster: the LEGO "Dam Breach" genre
- **Origin:** popularised by **King of Dams**. One 2019 video passed 31M views, and the genre went viral in 2023 (Know Your Meme: https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/lego-dam-breach-videos).
- **Copycats:** DangerousMoment, Best of Dam Experiments, Dam Breach, King of Dam Breach.
- **Title pattern:** "[Place in the mini city] Hit by Tsunami – Lego Dam Breach Experiment". Examples: Prison, Missile Silo, Nuclear Power Plant, Waterpark, Coastal Town, Train Bridge (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wTdw8dl3ygQ, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ODQS-kd8FJA, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOqDKAcPfIw, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_L_YDVTiLYI).
- **Structure:** the calm town first, then the wall of water, then the cascade, which they label "Experiment". The water is real, so the "is it real?" question works in their favour.
- **Spin-offs:** "Tidal Takedown" uses a wave machine. "Giant Tsunami Wave DESTROYS Mini City!" (https://www.yayafa.com/2929190/).
- **Lesson:** a named town location under a natural threat, presented as a test, is a franchise engine. The comment pull comes from "will X survive?".

## 2. Patterns across the niches

1. **The first frame states the threat or the contest.** Zack D. starts with something in peril, BeamNG with "vehicle vs obstacle", dam breach with a calm town plus a wall of water, Algodoo with "pick your country". No establishing shot without stakes.
2. **Titles follow a few repeatable shapes:**
   - "[Thing] vs [Obstacle]";
   - "[Place] Hit by [Natural force]";
   - "What happens if…";
   - "How does X work (in 40 seconds)";
   - "N [things] Elimination Race #37";
   - "No Traffic Lights in…" (one rule changed).
3. **Numbered series with one variable swapped** ("#65") lowers the cost of each idea and builds habit. Leaders post every day or several times a day (Zack D. 4–5 a day, Country League daily).
4. **A side to root for, in the first second** (country marbles, teams in Marble League, "will the prison survive?"). It drives comments ("my X won", "RIP the train").
5. **Escalation ladders.** The same test, bigger each time: car → truck → train, or 10 → 100 → 1000 cars.
6. **"Is it real?"** Real miniature content (Wunderland, LEGO dams) and the tilt-shift "real or miniature" Shorts gather comments arguing about whether it is real. CGI channels earn the same reaction with physical plausibility: water, debris, crowd flow.
7. **Order from chaos, or chaos from order.** Traffic games (Mini Motorways "zen", CS2 "no traffic lights") show a system breaking and then being fixed. Academic work on why miniatures appeal lists scaling effects, an illusion of control, admiration of craft and ideal worlds (Taylor & Francis, "Miniature appreciation": https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/21500894.2020.1797865). A psychologist quoted by Yahoo: we feel "a greater sense of control and power over smaller things" (https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/why-tiny-things-us-happy-200908534.html).
8. **Loops pay off directly.** Since 31 Mar 2025 a Shorts view counts on every start *and replay* (TechCrunch: https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted; Tubefilter: https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/). Monetisation and YPP still use "engaged views" (Search Engine Journal: https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/). A last frame that matches the first raises displayed views. A vendor claims loop rates above 25% average 180K views against 22K below 10% (https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026) **[vendor, unverified]**.
9. **Kid-heavy audiences cluster around vehicles plus destruction plus emoji titles** (BeamNG, Gecko's Garage). Adults cluster around "how a system works" and "satisfying engineering" (Zack D., Mini Motorways, CS2, port explainers).

## 3. What they do that Pocket City doesn't yet (concrete format changes)

1. **Put the threat or contest in frame 1.** Don't open on a calm aerial establishing shot. Open on the problem already visible: the ferry is already drifting, or the bridge is already rising with the bus 200 m away. The narrator's "You have 47 seconds" lands over a picture that *already* shows why.
2. **Title formulas.** Keep the "You have 47 seconds…" voice line, but test titles in proven shapes:
   - "Can 1 Tow Truck Clear 200 Cars Before the Bridge Opens?"
   - "What Happens If Every Traffic Light Turns Green?"
   - "[Town spot] vs [force]: Harbour vs 10 m Wave"
   - "No Traffic Lights in Pocket City for 60 s"
3. **Numbered, variable-swap series:**
   - "Pocket City Emergency #12";
   - "One Rule Changed" (no lights / one-way only / everyone drives on the left);
   - "Escalation" (1 → 10 → 100 broken-down buses);
   - "Can You Save the Town?"

   One series a week. Reuse the town and the rigs; change the crisis. Small changes between episodes do not by themselves trip the inauthentic content rule (see the risks below).
4. **A side to root for in second 1.** "Pick a tug: red or blue." "Which ambulance gets there first, the one through the tunnel or the one over the bridge?" Show an on-screen split route and a timer; the comments pick sides.
5. **A visible countdown and a progress map.** A diegetic timer, such as a clock tower or a harbour signal, plus a small inset map of the route. The countdown is the "race against time" made readable with the sound off.
6. **Escalation inside one film.** Three beats, each bigger: one car stalls → a bus jams the junction → the drawbridge is stuck half-open with the ferry arriving.
7. **An engineered loop.** The last frame equals the first: the town returns to the identical calm shot, or the final line ("…and tomorrow at 8:14, it starts again") rhymes with the opening. Each replay counts as a view since Mar 2025.
8. **Make "real simulation" visible.** Show that the traffic is simulated (a jam wave moving backwards, a car-following ripple). Optionally end with a one-line fact ("This is why phantom jams form": https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop18013/appd.htm). This gives the Zack-D-style "I learned something" payoff and separates us from AI tiny-world slop.
9. **A comment hook in the voice-over.** End with a decision, not a resolution, on some episodes: "Would you have sent the tug or the crane?" (the POV channels' "what would you do"). Or leave one dangling question: "Did you spot the cyclist who caused it?" That drives rewatches.
10. **"Breaking news" framing** (the Wunderland staged-emergency beat): a tiny lower-third "POCKET CITY NEWS: drawbridge stuck" as a variant opener.
11. **Cadence.** Leaders post every day. If the code pipeline allows, aim for 3–5 a week, built from reusable "crisis modules" (stuck bridge, power cut, fog, flood) rather than single bespoke films.
12. **Behind the simulation:** an occasional "how this Short was coded" or an overhead wireframe reveal, the "is it real?" reveal in reverse. Miniature and diorama audiences admire craft (one of the ten theses in the academic review).

### Risks
- **Made for Kids.** YouTube's factors include "characters, celebrities, or toys that appeal to children, including animated characters or cartoon figures", plus subject matter, music and visual style (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076). Animation alone doesn't decide it, but bright vehicle cartoons tip towards kids. If a video is set to MFK: no comments, no notification bell, no end screens, cards or playlists features, no personalised ads (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541). Creators are legally responsible for setting it themselves, under COPPA (FTC: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children).
  - **Mitigation:** keep adult-leaning framing (systems, time pressure, real-world engineering facts, an adult narrator, no sing-song music, no mascot characters, no "kids" or "for children" in metadata). Avoid emoji-heavy BeamNG-style kid titles. Answer the audience question honestly, and never set it to "not for kids" just to keep comments if kids are clearly the intended audience.
- **Inauthentic (formerly "repetitious") content.** Renamed 15 Jul 2025. It targets mass-produced or templated content with little variation, and enforcement is judged at channel level (https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/, https://umbrellacreators.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-edited-videos; third-party summaries). Blogs report a further split in July 2026, which is unverified.
  - **Mitigation:** each episode needs a distinct story, a distinct crisis and new narration, not the same template with a new colour. Series are fine; clones are not.
- **Commoditised look.** The AI "tiny world" and TikTok's native tilt-shift effect mean the look alone won't stand out. The edge is simulation plus story.

## 4. 30 story premises (town scale, machines, races; no fire, food, aliens, real places or brands)

Each is evidenced by a proven adjacent format (in brackets).

1. **Drawbridge stuck half-open.** You have 50 s to get a stalled bus across before the ferry arrives. [stuck mechanism + vehicle peril: BeamNG, Wunderland]
2. **Ambulance race, two routes.** Tunnel versus harbour bridge; viewers pick one in second 1. [choose-your-side: Algodoo races]
3. **Every traffic light turns green at once.** You are the traffic controller and have 40 s to restore order. [one rule changed: CS2 "No Traffic Lights"]
4. **Phantom jam.** One driver taps the brakes; follow the wave backwards and stop it before it reaches the hospital junction. [traffic science explainer: Dream Dive topic, FHWA]
5. **Storm surge on the sea wall.** Close the floodgates before the wave reaches the high street. [LEGO dam breach]
6. **Dam upstream cracks.** Evacuate the low town by bus, ferry and train in 60 s. [dam breach + evacuation logistics]
7. **Runaway container.** A crane drops a box onto the quay rails, and a freight train is 45 s out. [crane-collapse news Shorts, trains vs obstacle]
8. **Tugboat race.** Two tugs must turn a giant ship before it hits the pier. [escalation + port explainer]
9. **City-wide power cut at rush hour.** Lights go out block by block; route the repair truck. [system breaks and heals]
10. **Fog bank rolls in.** The ferry captain (you) has to dock blind using only the lighthouse. [second-person POV, "you are the…"]
11. **Escalation ladder.** 1 broken-down car → 10 → 100 → the whole bridge: how many tow trucks does Pocket City need? [BeamNG escalation, numbered]
12. **Snowstorm overnight.** You command three snowploughs and have to clear the hospital, school and harbour roads before 7:00. [time-lapse + machinery satisfaction]
13. **Water main burst.** The street floods and a sinkhole opens under a parked delivery van. [sinkhole disaster Shorts]
14. **Kite in the tram wires.** A kite string snags the overhead tram line, so the tram has to stop before the wire snaps. [chain reaction]
15. **Chain reaction town.** One rolling barrel on the quay sets off a Rube Goldberg chain through the whole harbour. [Joseph's Machines, marble runs]
16. **Marble-run city.** A giant ball rolls from the hilltop through every street to the sea; will it hit the ferry? [marble run + "will it survive"]
17. **Rush-hour merge.** You rebuild a junction live (roundabout versus lights versus zipper) and watch the queue melt. [Mini Motorways "ultimate roundabout"]
18. **Ship-to-shore relay.** A spare part has to go from a ship to the hospital: crane → truck → tram → bike courier, 60 s. [relay race]
19. **The 8:14 train is late.** One delay cascades through the bus connections; can you fix the timetable? [system cascade, Mini Metro]
20. **Container stack domino.** Wind topples one stack. [domino Shorts, crane-collapse news]
21. **Tidal race.** The causeway road floods at high tide in 45 s and the last bus is still on the island. [race against nature]
22. **Elevator / funicular stuck.** The hill railway stalls halfway, and you send a rescue crane. [machinery rescue]
23. **Giant cruise ship arrives and the town is too small.** Every road and tug has to rearrange. [scale contrast]
24. **A day in Pocket City in 40 s.** A time-lapse from 5:00 to 23:00, the town waking up, with one hidden crisis to spot. [Dream Dive "how a city wakes up"; rewatch hook]
25. **What happens if every car leaves at once?** A town evacuation drill. [what-if, Zack D.]
26. **Bridge weight test.** Trucks of rising size cross a new bridge: 1 t, 10 t, 40 t, the mobile crane. [vs-obstacle escalation]
27. **Runaway roller.** A roadworks roller rolls downhill towards the market square. [vehicle peril]
28. **Lighthouse failure at night.** Three ships, one rock; guide them in with the harbour searchlight. [night + countdown]
29. **Earthquake drill.** The ground shakes for 10 s; which buildings and cranes pass the test? [disaster sim, "will it survive"]
30. **Dropped crane hook.** A crane operator (you) has to place a bridge section into a gap before the tide turns. [satisfying placement, Townscaper and Mini Motorways precision]
31. **Bus driver on the last lap.** 20 stops, 60 s, every light against you. [POV "you are the bus driver"]
32. **Heat wave: the rail lines buckle.** Slow every train before the bend. [system rule change]
