# YouTube Shorts distribution and retention, late 2026
Research date: 2026-11-26. Method: 29 web searches (search-result summaries only; no page fetches were possible, so even "official" pages were seen via search snippets). For our use case: three small, faceless, code-animated English Shorts channels with TTS voice, 20 to 60 s vertical.

Strength tags:
- **fact**: official YouTube (Help Center, blog.youtube, Creator Insider) or major press, seen in 2 or more sources
- **data**: a study with a stated sample size
- **practice**: creator advice repeated across many sources, with no controlled evidence
- **unverified**: one source only, sources conflict, or the source was not traceable

Caveat: most Shorts "algorithm" content online is SEO blogs from vendors. Hard thresholds such as "70% viewed rate" are not official. YouTube publishes signals, not weights.

## Recommendations for our three channels

1. **Track the right numbers.** Ignore raw views. Each week, track **"chose to view" %, engaged views, avg % viewed (and whether it goes above 100%), subscribers per 1K engaged views**, and survey-sensitive signals (dislikes, "don't recommend").
2. **Frame 0 is the thumbnail.** Every Short opens on motion plus a bold on-screen hook line, with the TTS voice starting at frame 0 (no silence, no logo, no title card). Lead with the payoff or the question.
3. **Burn in captions** in large, high-contrast, style-consistent type. Many viewers watch with the sound off, and captions help a synthetic voice land.
4. **Length: 20 to 40 s by default**, up to ~55 s only when the story carries it. Length is close to neutral once channel size is controlled for; avg % viewed is what drops. Cut every beat that doesn't move the story.
5. **Design seamless loops**: the last shot or line flows back into the first (the code animation makes this easy: end pose = start pose). Always deliver the payoff before the loop point. Don't trick viewers into rewatching.
6. **Avoid the "inauthentic/mass-produced" pattern.** Each Short needs a distinct idea, original script and visible creative choices. Vary structure, not just the subject swapped into one template. Keep the three channels clearly distinct in niche and look. Never re-upload clips (Oct 2026 originality update). This matters more for us than for most because we are faceless, TTS-voiced and code-made.
7. **AI disclosure**: stylised animation with a generic TTS narrator does not need the "AI use" label. Turn it on if any Short shows realistic people, places or events, or a cloned real voice.
8. **Made for Kids**: set it honestly. If a cartoon channel is aimed at children it must be MFK (no comments, no personalised ads). If it is general-audience animation, write it for adults or teens (themes, vocabulary) so it plainly isn't child-directed.
9. **Cadence: 3 to 5 Shorts per week per channel, on a fixed schedule**, late afternoon or evening in the US time zone (test Thu–Sat at 4–7 pm ET), then go by Studio's "when your viewers are on YouTube". Quality per Short over volume. Don't delete underperformers; Shorts can resurface.
10. **Titles**: short, plain-language, searchable (search is ~9% of Shorts views), plus 2 to 4 relevant hashtags. #Shorts is optional.
11. **Covers**: when custom Shorts thumbnails reach our channels (YPP from Jul 2026; reportedly open to all since 23 Nov 2026, so check Studio), upload a designed cover for the channel grid and search. Until then, pick a strong in-video frame. Design one frame per Short to double as the cover.
