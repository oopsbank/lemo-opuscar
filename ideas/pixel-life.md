# Pixel Life: format and 50-idea backlog

2.5D pixel-art dioramas (`styles/hd-2d`), "You Are a …" job comedies where everything goes wrong,
and the last line echoes the first. Characters are pixel sprites, so no realistic humans. Made so
far: *Fish Seller*, *Pizza Delivery Guy*, *Night Museum Guard*. This list has **no food delivery,
no museum, no fish stall, no aliens**. Vehicles, town emergencies and fire belong to Pocket City.

The formula with timings is in [`FORMULA.md`](FORMULA.md#3-pixel-life). The research behind it is in
[`research/pixel-comedy.md`](research/pixel-comedy.md).

## 1. What the leaders do that we don't yet

| Leader (approx. scale) | What they do | What we do now | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alan's Universe** (~100M subs, almost all Shorts under 60 s) | One fixed hero, one fixed world, recurring side characters, "Part 2" serials | A new "you" every episode | **One recurring pixel hero** who takes and loses a new job every episode: "Job #4" |
| **Viva La Dirt League, *Epic NPC Man*** (5.7B views) | Shop NPCs with jobs suffer the same daft player; game logic applied to a day job | Game look, but no game logic | **Game HUD gags:** customer patience bar, a "QUEST: survive the lunch rush" banner, "GAME OVER — YOU'RE FIRED", XP counter |
| **Scott Seiss, "Angry Retail Guy"** (hundreds of millions of views) | Opens on the customer's dumb question, then the worker's deadpan reply | Opens on calm set-up narration | **Cold open on the customer's line** or on the disaster in mid-air |
| **Gus Johnson** ("Every Cat at 3 a.m.") | `Every X at [time]` and `Types of X` titles; escalation to absurdity | One title shape only | Test `POV: Day 1 as a …`, `Every … at 6 AM` and `You Are a …` (FORMULA) |
| **Pencilmation / Minecraft Shorts** (10–20M) | Readable with no sound; wordless sight gags | Voice-led | Make each gag read without sound; burn captions in a pixel font |
| **Live-action job skits** (nurse, server, retail) | Comments are workers recognising their own job ("this is too real") | — | End on a question that invites worker stories; pin "Which job next?" |

Gap found: **no animated job-comedy channel with a distinctive look owns this niche.** The animated
workplace-POV channels that exist are small and look generic or AI-made. Pixel diorama + one hero +
game grammar is unoccupied.

## 2. Format changes (summary)

1. **A recurring hero.** Keep the second-person narration ("You are a…"), but always draw the same sprite: one silhouette and one colour no set uses (e.g. a short guy with a cowlick and an oversized red lanyard). Episode counter: `Job #4`. Optional running gag: he gets fired at the end and the last frame is his next application form.
2. **A recurring side cast:** **The Customer** (one entitled NPC with a "!" bubble who turns up in every job), **The Manager**, **The Cat**. Running gags reward people who binge the channel ("not HIM again").
3. **Cold open:** frame 1 is the customer's absurd line, or the disaster in mid-air. Then "You are a hotel receptionist. Six hours earlier…". No calm establishing shot first.
4. **Length:** keep 55–61 s only for flagship episodes. Make **40–45 s the default** with a new gag every 5–7 s. Cut the set-up narration before the first gag.
5. **Game grammar** as the house style: HUD, quest banner, patience bar, "YOU'RE FIRED" game-over card. This is RPG nostalgia without copying any game, and it reads with the sound off.
6. **Loop:** the last line flows grammatically into the first ("…and that's why you are a hotel receptionist." → "You are a hotel receptionist.").
7. **Comments:** end with a worker-story question ("Receptionists: what's the worst you've had?"); pin "Which job should he get fired from next?"; make the most-asked job next.
8. **Seasonal slots:** retail and restaurant rudeness peaks in Nov–Dec. Black Friday is 27 Nov 2026, too soon to make, so aim for the **returns desk (26 Dec)** and **Mall Santa (December)** now.
9. **Cover frame:** a tight crop of the sprite plus the disaster, a 2–4 word caption ("DAY 1. FIRED?"); the tilt-shift blur leaves room for text at the top and bottom.

## 3. Ranking

**Score = Viral × Feasible** (each 1–5).
- **Viral** weighs: how many viewers have done or met the job (comment fuel); a funny line in frame 1; escalation; season fit.
- **Feasible** weighs: one diorama set, at most ~6 sprites, gags that pixel sprites and lights can sell (no crowds of 100, no detailed faces).

★ means the topic is proven viral in live-action job comedy (see research).
