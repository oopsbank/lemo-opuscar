# Some Crazy Thingzz — idea backlog

2D cartoon characters in 3D sets, 20–40 s (aim 20–30 s: research favours the short end, README §1),
"Why do …?" questions about everyday objects: a funny wrong answer acted out, then the clear real
one. Research and ranking method: [`README.md`](README.md).

Already made: mailbox red flag, crackers' holes; next up: golf-ball dimples. **One object per
film; no vehicles-in-town (Pocket City) and no job comedies (Pixel Life).** Facts must be true and
simple — check each against two sources when scripting; a wrong fact turns the comments against us.

Format reminders: frame 1 = the object, huge, with the question as on-screen text and spoken; the
wrong theory starts by second 2; the real answer lands with a visual demo; the last line asks the
next silly question (loop + comments).

Score = Viral × Feasible (each 1–5).

## Top 10

1. **Why Do Pens Have a Hole in the Cap?** — V5 × F5 = 25
   - Wrong idea: it's a tiny flute / a window for the pen to breathe.
   - Real answer: a safety design — if someone swallows a cap, air can still pass through it.
   - Hook: A cap zooming at camera, whistling through its hole.
   - Why: everyone has chewed one; surprising, slightly alarming answer = shares and comments.
2. **Why Do Jeans Have a Tiny Pocket?** — V5 × F5 = 25
   - Wrong idea: a pocket for a hamster / a mouse's sleeping bag.
   - Real answer: it was made for pocket watches.
   - Hook: A hamster climbing into the tiny pocket. "What is this pocket FOR?"
   - Why: the most famous "why" question of its kind; instant recognition. (Generic jeans, no brand.)
3. **Why Is There a Star on the Elevator Button?** — V4 × F5 = 20
   - Wrong idea: it's the VIP floor where celebrities live.
   - Real answer: it marks the main exit floor (ground/lobby) for emergencies.
   - Hook: A character pressing the star and getting a red carpet that isn't there.
   - Why: noticed by everyone, known by few; simple elevator set.
4. **Why Do Pencils Have Six Sides?** — V4 × F5 = 20
   - Wrong idea: so bees think it's a honeycomb.
   - Real answer: so it doesn't roll off the desk, and it fits the fingers' grip.
   - Hook: A round pencil rolling off a desk into the abyss, a scream.
   - Why: demo is trivial and satisfying (round rolls, hex stops).
5. **Why Do Airplane Windows Have a Tiny Hole?** — V5 × F4 = 20
   - Wrong idea: for passing notes to the pilot / for the plane to breathe.
   - Real answer: the breather hole balances pressure between the panes so the outer pane takes
     the load, and keeps the window from fogging.
   - Hook: A cartoon eye pressed to the hole: "Hello? Anyone out there?"
   - Why: huge curiosity question; only a window set needed (no real airline).
6. **Why Do Tennis Balls Have Fuzz?** — V4 × F5 = 20
   - Wrong idea: so they don't feel cold.
   - Real answer: the felt adds drag and grip, so the ball slows and spins predictably.
   - Hook: A shivering bald tennis ball.
   - Why: direct sibling of golf-ball dimples: a "sports balls" mini-series.
7. **Why Do Escalators Have Brushes on the Sides?** — V4 × F4 = 16
   - Wrong idea: to polish your shoes.
   - Real answer: they're skirt deflectors keeping shoes and clothes away from the gap.
   - Hook: A character holding a shoe to the brushes for a shine.
   - Why: everyone has wondered; easy 3D set.
8. **Why Do Bubble Wrap Bubbles Exist? (It Was Wallpaper)** — V4 × F4 = 16
   - Wrong idea: invented for popping.
   - Real answer: it started as a failed textured wallpaper, then became packing material.
   - Hook: Pop, pop, pop — in frame 1, with pop sounds on the beat.
   - Why: satisfying sound + a surprising origin; ASMR loop.
9. **Why Do Keyboards Have Bumps on F and J?** — V4 × F5 = 20
   - Wrong idea: they're the buttons for Fun and Joy.
   - Real answer: they let you find the home row by touch without looking.
   - Hook: A finger touching the bump, a dramatic "*gasp*."
   - Why: you can check it right now on your own keyboard → interaction.
10. **Why Do Bananas Have Brown Spots?** — V4 × F4 = 16
    - Wrong idea: the banana got a tan / it's a leopard banana.
    - Real answer: as it ripens, starch turns to sugar and enzymes brown the skin.
    - Hook: A banana putting on sunglasses.
    - Why: food + character; harmless, cute.

## Notes

- Title pattern is the searched question itself: `Why Do <Objects> Have <Thing>?`. No extra words.
- Series hooks: "sports balls" (dimples → fuzz → hexagons), "holes" (pen cap, airplane window,
  lollipop, pot handle), "mystery marks" (F/J bumps, gas arrow, toothpaste squares).
- Never repeat the same answer shape twice in a row (e.g. "holes help air flow").
