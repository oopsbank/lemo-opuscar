# Some Crazy Thingzz: format and 50-idea backlog

2D cartoon characters in simple 3D sets ask "Why do …?" about one everyday object, act out a funny
wrong theory, then give the clear true answer. Made so far: mailbox red flag, crackers' holes; next up:
golf-ball dimples. **One object per film.** Vehicles in town belong to Pocket City and job comedy
to Pixel Life.

The formula with timings is in [`FORMULA.md`](FORMULA.md#4-some-crazy-thingzz). The research is in
[`research/why-explainers.md`](research/why-explainers.md), and every answer below was fact-checked in
[`research/verify-objects.md`](research/verify-objects.md): the **chk** column gives the row number
there. Use the script line from that file, which is accurate and brand-free.

Gap found: the single-object "why" niche is **full of tiny, faceless AI channels with no breakout
brand**. Nobody has a **cast with a comic wrong theory**. Accuracy plus characters is the moat, and it
also guards against the "inauthentic content" and AI-slop pressure.

## 2. Format changes (summary)

1. **Length 25–35 s**, about 70–95 words of script. The answer lands by ~25 s.
2. **Frame 0:** the object huge, its odd detail circled, the question as text and voice at once.
3. **Fixed cast:** the confidently wrong one and the one who knows. Keep their current names if they have them.
4. **Signature moments:** the wrong one's opening line, the **❌ WRONG** stamp, the "Actually…" sting.
5. **Show, don't tell:** one demo per answer (a round pencil rolls off, a hexagonal one stops).
6. **Loop line:** the wrong one turns to the next object, or asks the same question again. Hard cut.
7. **Go one layer deeper** than the listicles (pen cap → pen barrel; "…and a safety standard asks for it").
8. **Series:** Holes in Things · Sports Balls · Road Stuff · Kitchen Secrets · Myth Busted.
9. **Comments:** "What did YOU think it was for?" Pin "Which object next?"
10. **Facts:** every answer checked against two sources, with sources in the description. Never invent an error to bait comments.

## 3. Ranking

**Score = Viral × Feasible** (each 1–5).
- **Viral** weighs: everyone owns or has seen the object; a surprising or "I always thought…" answer; shareable usefulness (a tip you can use today); a funny wrong theory; how worn-out the fact is (common facts lose a point unless we add a deeper layer).
- **Feasible** weighs: a simple 3D prop and set, an answer that can be *shown* in one demo, and a fact rated solid in the fact check (contested answers lose points).
