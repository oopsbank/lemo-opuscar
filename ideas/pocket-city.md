# Pocket City — idea backlog

Tilt-shift miniature harbour town (`styles/tilt-shift`), 35–45 s, second-person narrator ("You are
the bridge keeper…", "You have ninety seconds…"), chain reactions and races against time seen from
above, smooth camera, time-lapse stutter in the world. People are colour capsules, so no realistic
humans. Research and ranking method: [`README.md`](README.md).

Already made: *Engine Nine* (fire engine to a fire), *Ten Minutes* (pizza scooter), *Not In Our
Town* (UFO). So: **no fire, no food delivery, no aliens** here.

Recurring devices that fit the style and the research: the on-screen time-lapse clock (`×24` →
`×1 REAL TIME` when it matters), a countdown in the signage, one saturated hero in the sharp band,
the cut from wide to the one thing about to go wrong, an ending on the same aerial as the opening (loop).

Score = Viral × Feasible (each 1–5).

## Top 10

1. **The Drawbridge Won't Close** — V5 × F5 = 25
   - Story: You run the harbour drawbridge; a tall-masted ship is late, a wedding car convoy (just
     colour capsules + ribbons) and the morning bus queue both pile up on each side while you
     time the lift to the second.
   - Hook: Bridge half-raised, mast inching under it, clock `00:47` in the signage: "You have 47
     seconds and one bridge."
   - Why: one clear binary stake, satisfying mechanical motion, traffic sim is native to the style;
     loop on the bridge closing = opening frame.
2. **One Stuck Ferry Ramp** — V5 × F5 = 25
   - Story: The car ferry's ramp jams; cars back up through the whole town, a domino of
     roundabouts locks; one tow truck threads the gridlock to free it before the last sailing.
   - Hook: A wide of the whole town frozen in one long red snake, then the camera dives to the
     jammed ramp.
   - Why: "watch gridlock untangle" is pure satisfying content; the time-lapse unknotting is the
     payoff.
3. **The Runaway Shopping Trolley** — V5 × F4 = 20
   - Story: A trolley escapes the hilltop market and rolls down through the town; each near miss
     triggers the next (a cat, a ladder, a fruit stall, a mooring line) until it ends up in the
     ferry exactly as it leaves.
   - Hook: Trolley tipping over the top of the hill, "Somebody forgot one thing."
   - Why: classic Rube-Goldberg chain, hidden gags in the background for rewatches.
4. **Midnight Snow, 6 a.m. Opening** — V4 × F5 = 20
   - Story: Surprise overnight snow; you command three ploughs and a salt truck to clear every
     road before the school bell, the time-lapse showing the white streets turn black lane by lane.
   - Hook: Total white town, one plough's headlights switching on, "Six hours. Three ploughs."
   - Why: the clearing-pattern reveal is "oddly satisfying" (paths revealed), and seasonal (post
     in December).
5. **The Lighthouse Goes Dark** — V4 × F5 = 20
   - Story: Fog and the lighthouse lamp fails; a fishing boat is out; you run a chain of
     improvised lights (car headlights on the pier, every window switched on) to guide it home.
   - Hook: Lighthouse beam stutters and dies; one tiny boat light in grey fog.
   - Why: emotional stakes, lights are the style's strength at dusk, finale = the whole town lit.
6. **Market Day in 40 Seconds** — V4 × F5 = 20
   - Story: Empty square at 4 a.m. to a full market by 8, then packed away by noon: you are the
     organiser who has to fit one last stall before the bell.
   - Hook: Empty square, ×240 clock appears, the first van arrives.
   - Why: the time-lapse "build and unbuild" is the most native tilt-shift trick; loops perfectly
     (empty → empty).
7. **The Crane and the Wind** — V4 × F4 = 16
   - Story: A container crane must unload the last box before a storm front; gusts swing the box;
     everyone below scatters in rhythm.
   - Hook: Container swinging over tiny capsule people: "Wind's at forty. Box is at forty tons."
   - Why: suspense of a swinging load, heavy machinery appeals to the same audience as fire engines.
8. **Every Light in Town Goes Out** — V4 × F4 = 16
   - Story: A blackout at rush hour; traffic lights die; you, the one traffic officer, unknot a
     four-way crossroads by hand while a repair van races across town.
   - Hook: The town at dusk blinking off district by district.
   - Why: night look, lights-on payoff is a huge visual release; universally relatable.
9. **The Last Train Before the Tide** — V4 × F4 = 16
   - Story: A little coastal train must cross the causeway before high tide covers the tracks;
     sheep on the line, a stuck level crossing.
   - Hook: Water creeping over the rails, train whistle off-screen.
   - Why: rising water is an instantly readable clock; model-railway fans are a core miniature audience.
10. **The Ice Cream Truck vs. the Heatwave** — V4 × F4 = 16
    - Story: Hottest day of the year; the ice cream truck's freezer is failing; it has to sell out
      across town before everything melts; a route optimised like a puzzle.
    - Hook: A thermometer on a building ticking past 38 °C, a drip falling from the truck.
    - Why: sunny saturated colours, a cute stakes clock (melting), summer evergreen. (Not food
      delivery: it's a race against melting, no orders.)

## Notes

- Strongest series shape: **"one thing breaks, the whole town feels it"**; the narrator is the one
  person who fixes it. Name each by the broken thing.
- Every film opens and closes on the same high oblique; the stutter rate locks to the beat.
- One hidden gag per film in the blur band (a cat on every roof, the same balloon somewhere).
