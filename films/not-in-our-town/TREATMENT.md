# Not In Our Town — treatment

Style: Tilt-Shift Miniature. YouTube Short for Pocket City, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 40 s, smooth motion (v3, no stepping). English voice and subtitles, narrator Kokoro `am_michael` (the channel's voice), short second-person lines. Same harbour town as *Engine Nine* and *Ten Minutes*; Engine 9 returns.

User's brief: an alien spaceship attacks the town; the town fights back and drives it off (clear, satisfying win); no storyboard.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Invasion and counter-attack (chosen).** The shadow, the reveal, the abductions (car, palm tree, sailboat), the alarm at Engine 9, the town rallying (engine + fireboats), the water attack, the saucer gives everything back and flees. A new event every 3–5 s; one villain, one goal (stop it), one turn (the water hits), a clean win.
2. **The saucer's point of view.** A tour of the toy town through the saucer's beam as it "collects" souvenirs; the town only fights back at the end. Funny, but the town is passive for 30 s and the win is rushed.
3. **One day, interrupted.** A locked vantage over the quay, ordinary time-lapse day, then the saucer and the fight all in the same frame. Clean native move, but one frame can't show the alarm, the rally or the scale.

## 2. Logline and arc

A flying saucer parks over Pocket City's harbour and starts beaming up cars, trees and boats, until Engine 9 and the fireboats hose it down.

Setup (a shadow that isn't a cloud; the reveal) → escalation (car, palm tree, sailboat taken) → response (alarm, Engine 9 rolls, fireboats sail) → the turn (water hits the rim lights in slow motion, sparks) → resolution (in real time, it drops everything back where it was and bolts) → the town cheers; time-lapse resumes as the camera rises.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Tilt-shift time-lapse films** in general. Learn: speed changes as story beats, the cloud shadow as weather crossing a model. Don't take: places, shots, music.
- **1950s flying-saucer B-movies** as a genre (the round shadow, the hum, the beam, the sliding-tone "theremin" sound). Learn: the shadow-before-reveal, the rising beam, the wobble before it flees. Don't take: any film's ship design, shots or music.

## 4. HUD

The channel's speed plate, top left (y ≈ 140–290): a clock face whose hand spins with the world clock and the label `×12 TIME-LAPSE` / `×1 REAL TIME` / `×¼ SLOW-MO`. Under it, a small saucer-alert line ("UFO OVERHEAD", red, blinking) from the reveal to the escape: part of the joke, read at a glance.

## 6. Cue map

- 120 BPM. D minor under the threat (shots 1–5), D major from the hit (shot 6, 30.0) to the end.
- Saucer motif: a sliding sine "theremin" (synthesized, vibrato, glide), three notes falling a minor third. Heard at the reveal and every time the beam fires.
- Shot 1: marimba pulse in 16ths, pizzicato bass; at 2.5 a tape-stop into silence (only the hum).
- Shot 2: silence + hum + theremin motif + one low gong at the reveal.
- Shot 3: pulse back, minor; bowed vibraphone on each abduction, timpani on each new target.
- Shot 4: alarm bell; brass stabs, snare.
- Shot 5: full ensemble, marimba + xylophone + horns, building.
- Shot 6: timpani roll into the slow-mo; silence in the slow-mo except water hiss and sparks; at 30.0 a horn chord in D major.
- Shot 7: real time, score sparse: single pizzicato notes on each landing (car, palm, boat), a glissando whoosh as the saucer leaves.
- Shot 8: full major theme on marimba, glockenspiel and horns; resolve on D at 39.
