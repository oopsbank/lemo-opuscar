# Two Tugs, One Giant Ship — treatment

Style: Tilt-Shift Miniature. Pocket City, YouTube Short, 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~30 s, smooth motion, golden-hour look (as All Green). Idea #11 of `ideas/pocket-city.md`, series **Pick a Side #1**, built to `ideas/FORMULA.md` and the manager's retention notes (frame 0 = the problem big and close, big text in the first second, 25–32 s, loop).

## Three candidate structures
1. **Pick red or blue (chosen).** A huge cruise ship drifts sideways toward the harbour quay in a gust (frame 0: its white wall sliding at the camera, people on the quay, "PIER IN 0:40"). Two tugs, RED and BLUE, race in; the viewer picks one in second 1. Each tug has a push meter. Beats: red pushes the bow, blue takes the stern line; the blue line snaps; a sailboat cuts across red's path; both shove together in real time and the ship stops 2 m from the pier at 0:02. The meters end almost level; red edges it. Question: "Red or blue: who did more?"
2. **One tug, three ships.** Escalation (small ferry, freighter, cruise ship). Clear but no side to pick.
3. **The ship turns itself.** A thruster demo; no rivalry, weaker hook.

## Payoff fact
At low speed a ship's rudder hardly works (it needs water flowing past it), so big ships need tugs to turn and berth in tight harbours.
