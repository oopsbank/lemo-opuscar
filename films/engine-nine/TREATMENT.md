# Engine Nine — treatment

Style: Tilt-Shift Miniature. Format: YouTube Short, 1080×1920 vertical, 24 fps, 37 s. Language: English (voice, subtitles, signs). Narrator: relaxed male voice (Kokoro `am_michael`), second person.

Shorts safe zones: nothing that must be read below y ≈ 1500 or in the right-hand 140 px (YouTube's caption, channel name and buttons sit there); the HUD sits under the top 120 px.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The run (chosen).** One continuous journey from the station to the fire. The sharp band rides the red truck the whole way; each obstacle is a hard drop from time-lapse to real time, so the film's pulse *is* the truck's progress. Ends on the fire going out in real time.
2. **Countdown board.** A straight top-down of the town as a game board with an ETA counter; we only dive into oblique views at the obstacles. Clear, but it keeps us far from the truck and turns the film into a map.
3. **Two clocks.** Split the town: the fire grows in time-lapse on one side while the truck crawls in real time on the other, converging at the end. Strong idea, but a vertical frame split in two makes both halves tiny.

Why 1: the vertical frame shows a long stretch of road ahead of the truck — perfect for a chase; one subject, one goal, and the native move (the speed drop) lands on every obstacle.

## 2. Logline and arc

You drive Engine Nine across a harbour town that looks like a toy, and the town keeps getting in your way.

- **Setup**: alarm, smoke already visible over the roofs, truck out.
- **Turn**: gridlock parts, then the drawbridge stops everything — real time, silence, waiting.
- **Ending**: the bridge drops, the truck arrives, the ladder climbs, the water hits; the last flame dies in real time and the town goes quiet.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Keith Loutit's tilt-shift time-lapse films** (e.g. the beach-rescue piece *Bathtub IV*). Learn: emergency vehicles read as toys and the stakes still land; the time-lapse/real-time contrast as storytelling; long lenses from rooftops. Don't take: any location, shot, edit or music.
- **Classic chase-sequence editing** (a single vehicle, obstacles in rising order, a forced stop before the finish). Learn: escalation and the held "wait" beat before release. Don't take: specific gags or shots.

## 4. World

A whitewashed hillside harbour town: white and cream walls, cobalt shutters, terracotta roofs, very green palms and squares, dark asphalt, turquoise water. The **only saturated red** is the fire engine (and later the flames). Low to mid-rise buildings so the ground always shows. A canal with a drawbridge cuts the town from the harbour warehouses. Smoke column (dark grey) rises from a terracotta warehouse by the water the whole time; at a 45–55° pitch it stays out of the early shots, so the audience first sees it at 22.0, beyond the raised bridge — the destination revealed at the moment the truck is stuck.

Fire station: a small building with a big roller door; "ENGINE 9" painted on its flat roof (the title lives in the world).

## 6. Beat sheet (120 bpm, 1 beat = 0.5 s, 1 bar = 2 s)

- 0.0 alarm bell; HUD `×12 TIME-LAPSE`; band on station. VO1 0.3 "You drive Engine Nine."
- 1.0–2.0 roller door rises in steps; 2.5 truck noses out, light bar flashing.
- 2.4 VO2 "Smoke down by the harbor. Go."
- 4.0 cut: truck on the hill road; cars pull aside, each on an 8 Hz step (each one gates a marimba note).
- 8.0 cut: palm boulevard; truck overtakes a bus.
- 12.0 cut: crossroads jammed. 12.4 VO3 "Gridlock." HUD ramps to `×4`.
- 14.0 / 15.0 / 16.0 three pairs of cars hop aside, one pair per beat-pair, opening a lane; siren whoop on each.
- 16.6 VO4 "Move, move!" ; 17.0 truck surges through.
- 18.0 cut to top-down; ×12; truck races the harbour road (corners on beats).
- 22.0 cut: bridge up, sailboat crossing. **Hard drop to ×1**: tape-stop, music out. Real sound: gulls, lapping water, idling engine.
- 23.0 VO5 "Bridge is up."
- 24.5 boat clears. 25.0–27.5 bridge lowers (real time, smooth).
- 27.5 clunk (first sound after the silence). 28.0 music slams back.
- 28.0 cut: warehouse wide; truck arrives 28.8; outriggers 29.5; ladder climbs in steps 29.5–30.5; water on 30.5.
- 31.5 cut: roof close. Flames shrink one step per beat; smoke greys → white steam.
- 34.0 last flame out → **×1**, silence, steam hiss.
- 34.4 VO6 "Fire's out. Nobody's hurt." One glockenspiel note. Hold to 36.5.

## 7. Cue map

- Tempo 120 bpm, 4/4, D major. Bars 1–18 (36 s) plus a 0.5 s tail.
- S1 bars 1–2: alarm motif (glockenspiel + tubular bells, hi–lo siren interval A–D), woodblock tick.
- S2/S3 bars 3–6: marimba ostinato (phase pattern), voices added every bar: kalimba, vibraphone; each car that pulls aside gates a vibraphone note of the skeleton.
- S4 bars 7–9: ostinato thins to marimba + low pulse; three tuned horn/brass chords on the partings (14.0, 15.0, 16.0); full return at 17.0.
- S5 bars 10–11: everything, celesta counter-line, corners on downbeats.
- S6 bars 12–14: tape-stop at 22.0 (varispeed down 0.4 s) → silence (real sound only) → nothing musical until 28.0.
- S7 bars 15–18: full ensemble 28.0–31.5; from 31.5 one voice drops per beat-pair as flames shrink; 34.0 silence; 34.4 single glockenspiel D6, ring out.

## 8. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alarm 0–4 | sped-up town hiss (granular) | alarm bell, door rattle steps, engine start | alarm motif |
| Run 4–12 | sped-up hiss | siren (low in mix), relay clicks on stutter steps | marimba builds |
| Gridlock 12–18 | hiss fades as speed falls | horns (2), siren whoops, engine rev at 17.0 | thin → full |
| Top-down 18–22 | hiss | siren doppler | full |
| Bridge 22–28 | gulls, water lapping, harbour wind | engine idle, boat horn 23.8, bridge motor hum, clunk 27.5 | silence |
| Fire 28–34 | fire crackle, harbour wind | air brakes, outriggers thump, ladder ratchet steps, water hiss | full → thinning |
| End 34–36.5 | steam hiss, distant gulls | water drips | one glock note |

At least two silences: 22.0–27.5 and 34.0–34.4. Sound as transition: the gull bed fades in 0.3 s before the 22.0 cut (J-cut); fire crackle starts under 27.5 before the cut to the warehouse.

## 9. Subtitles and type

- Subtitles are road-sign panels: dark green rounded rectangle, thin white inner border, white **Overpass Bold** (OFL), centred at x = 540, inside the lower blur band at y ≈ 1340 (above the Shorts caption zone). Each line held ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
- HUD: top-left at (60, 150): `×12 TIME-LAPSE` / `×1 REAL TIME` in Overpass Mono on a small dark plate, with a tiny clock face whose hand spins with the speed.
- Title: "ENGINE 9" painted on the station roof (shot 1). No end card; the last image is the quiet roof.

## 10. Changes made during production

- Time-lapse reads ×12 (×3 in the gridlock crawl, ×1 at the bridge and at the end); ×30 moved the ambient traffic too fast to read in a vertical frame.
- Shot 1 is seen from the south-east so the red roller door and the painted roof title both read; shot 3 runs head-on toward the camera.
- Subtitles: white glazed street-name tiles with a cobalt border and cobalt Overpass (the harbour town's own signs), a small red beacon that blinks until the fire is out; centred at y = 1395, above the Shorts caption zone.
- Last line changed to "Fire's out. Nobody's hurt." (the speech check heard "Everyone's safe" as "everyone safe"); the film runs to 37.0 s so it holds long enough to read.
- Demo check (after the treatment): structure (a single run vs a town waking up), opening (alarm + station vs a dawn light trail), signature moment (the forced wait at the raised bridge vs a dive onto a crossing), camera path and ending (the last flame out in real time, no rise) all differ; the rotating top-down is the one shared move.
