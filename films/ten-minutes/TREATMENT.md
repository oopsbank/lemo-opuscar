# Ten Minutes — treatment

Style: Tilt-Shift Miniature. YouTube Short, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 45 s, smooth motion (no time-lapse stepping, at the user's request). English voice and subtitles; narrator Kokoro `am_michael` (the channel's voice from *Engine Nine*), second person. Same harbour town as *Engine Nine*.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The obstacle run (chosen).** One rider, one pizza, a ten-minute countdown, six obstacles in rising order, each a different kind of trouble (crowd, animal, machine, ritual, the town itself, weather). The user asked for "more things happening": the run gives a new event every 4–6 s.
2. **Split clocks.** The pizza cooling on one side of the screen, the rider on the other. Clear stakes, but the vertical frame makes two halves too small.
3. **Rewind.** Open on the hand-over at 9:59, then rewind the whole trip at ×60. A fun trick, but it spends the suspense up front.

## 2. Logline and arc

You have ten minutes to get a hot pizza across a toy-sized harbour town, and the town throws everything it has at you.

Setup (oven, timer starts) → escalation (market, dog, roadworks) → the forced wait (a parade, in real time, the clock ticking second by second) → the stunt (a jump over a rising drawbridge, in slow motion) → the last insult (rain) → the hand-over to a customer on a boat at 9:59 (0:01 left), sun out, a rainbow over the harbour.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Tilt-shift time-lapse films** in general (high vantage, the blur band, speed changes as storytelling). Learn: the drop to real time as the emotional beat. Don't take: any place, shot or music.
- **Classic chase comedy** (obstacles that get sillier, a stunt before the finish, a deadpan ending). Learn: escalation and timing of the beat before each release. Don't take: specific gags.

## 4. The clock

Top-left plate: a big countdown `MM:SS` (Overpass Mono) and under it the speed (`×18 TIME-LAPSE`, `×1 REAL TIME`, `×¼ SLOW-MO`). The countdown is driven by the same sim clock as the world, so it races in time-lapse and ticks one second per second in real time. It stops at `00:01` at the hand-over and turns green.

## 6. Cue map

- 120 BPM, D major (the channel's key), scooter motif on marimba + xylophone in 16ths, pizzicato bass, woodblock.
- Each obstacle has its own colour: market = kalimba + shaker; dog = playful pizzicato; roadworks = low brass stabs; parade = diegetic marching band (snare, bass drum, tuba, trumpets) in real time, the score silent; bridge = timpani roll → silence in the slow-motion jump → crash on landing; rain = vibraphone, softer; arrival = glockenspiel, the score resolving on D at 41.5.
