## Three candidate structures
1. **Four gates, one clock (chosen).** A storm surge is coming in from the sea; the harbour's sea wall has four openings with red steel flood gates. Close them in order, each harder than the last: the big canal mouth (easy), the ferry ramp (a van parked in it, tow it), the boat ramp (the surge in sight), the fish-market gap (closes at 0:02 in real time as the wave hits). The wall holds; the sea stands higher than the quay on the outside, the canal stays low on the inside.
2. **Will the wall hold?** Fixed gates, the camera just watches the sea rise up the wall with a gauge. Clear "will it survive" but no hero and nothing to do.
3. **Pick a side: gate or sandbags.** Two quays, two defences, split screen. Splits shrink everything in a vertical frame, and the debate is weaker than an order-of-gates question.

## Fact check (payoff line)
A storm surge is an abnormal rise of the sea generated by a storm, over and above the predicted tide, mostly caused by strong winds pushing water onshore, with a smaller part from low air pressure: NOAA National Hurricane Center, "Storm Surge Overview" (nhc.noaa.gov/surge); UK Met Office, "What is a storm surge?" (metoffice.gov.uk). Floodgates in sea walls ("flood gates" in a "sea defence") are closed before high water: a common harbour defence (e.g. Environment Agency flood gate operations).
