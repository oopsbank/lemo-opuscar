# You Are a Fish Seller — treatment

Style: 2.5D Pixel Art (`styles/hd-2d/STYLE.md`). Vertical 1080×1920, English voice and subtitles, ~55 s.
Brief: you (second person) run a fish stall at a Western harbour market; one funny situation after another; narrator + in-world dialogue boxes; no logo; no storyboard stop.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. The day-cycle stall.** One set, dawn to dusk in 55 s. Each hour a new customer and a new disaster, lit by different practicals (lamp, noon sun, sunset, string lights). Ending: night, lamp on, one sale. *Strong on light; weak on escalation (the gags don't connect, it's a list).*

**B. The domino.** One sale sets off a single chain reaction across the whole market (a gull, a cart, a barrel, a boat), one continuous tracking shot. *Great signature shot, but a 55 s single chain is hard to read at phone size, and there are no beats for a narrator.*

**C. The rule book that breaks (chosen).** The narrator reads "the rules of being a fish seller" as RPG chapter cards — RULE I to RULE IV — and each rule is broken at once by what happens. The gags are causally chained: the gull you greet steals a fish; the grandma out-haggles you; the fish on the scale is not dead and escapes; while you chase it, the gull takes over your stall. Opening line "You are a fish seller." comes back at the end as "You were a fish seller."

Why C: chapter cards are native RPG grammar and give a vertical short a clear beat every ~10 s; the four rules escalate (lose a fish → lose money → lose the catch → lose the job); the ending echoes the opening line and the opening framing, changed. Opening image from the topic: a dark stall where one lamp clicks on and a man holds up a fish. Score shape from the topic: a small sea-shanty jig that stumbles (stops dead at every punchline) and finally gets taken over too — the last statement of the tune plays at the gull's tempo.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline:** At dawn on a foggy harbour, you open your fish stall determined to follow the rules — and lose, in order, a fish, your money, your catch and your stall, to a seagull.

- **Setup** (0–6 s): dark pier, the stall lamp clicks on, you hold up a fish, proud. "You are a fish seller." RULE I.
- **Escalation** (6–37 s): gull steals a fish; grandma leaves with the fish *and* your coins; the "dead" fish says "Nope." and flops into the sea while you chase it.
- **Turn** (37–43 s): you turn round: the gull stands behind your counter in your yellow cap, taking the grandma's money. RULE IV: "Never leave your stall."
- **Ending** (43–55 s): the sun rises over the harbour; rising pull-back to the whole market; the sharp band narrows onto you, sitting on a crate, a customer now. "You were a fish seller." Title card.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Untitled Goose Game* (House House, 2019): **learn** how one animal ruins one person's routine in readable, single-action gags, staged in a high 3/4 view of a tidy little world; the score that stops dead on a gag. **Don't take** the goose, its design, the garden, the piano score or any gag.
- *Shaun the Sheep* (Aardman): **learn** the deadpan reaction hold — a stare long enough to be funny — and anticipation before every pratfall. **Don't take** characters, designs, music.

## 4. Characters and sets

- **You** — fishmonger, ~44 px sprite, **lemon-yellow oilskin cap and apron** (the one saturated colour; the set is blue fog, grey boards, amber lamp). Poses: stand, hold-fish-up, wave, talk, outraged (arms up), hand-over, run (4 frames), skid, sit-slump.
- **Gull** — white/grey, orange beak; poses: fly (2), land, stand, squawk (beak open), grab, wearing-cap.
- **Grandma** — small, violet shawl, white bun, a shopping basket; poses: walk (2), stand, two-fingers, stare (eyes narrow), leave.
- **The Fish** — big silver-blue cod-ish fish with one huge eye; poses: lie (eye shut), eye open, flop (2), leap.
- **Set: one harbour market pier at dawn.** Boardwalk on piles over pixel water, your stall (counter of ice with fish, awning, a scale, cash tin, a lamp on a pole), two neighbouring stalls shut (dark), stacked crates and barrels, rope coils, moored boats, far quay with houses and lit windows, fog. Key: cold blue-grey pre-dawn ambient; practicals: your stall lamp (amber), a few far windows, boat lanterns. Late in the film the sun rises (pale gold key from low right, fog thins pink).

## 7. Cue map

- **Tempo**: 6/8, dotted-quarter 90 (bar 1.333 s). Chase section at dotted-quarter 120 (bar 1.0 s). Ending at 72 (ritardando into the title).
- **Instruments** (style palette, folk side): recorder (melody), plucked lute/guitar (pluck.py), hand drum + tambourine, pizzicato strings and a bassoon for the grandma, harp + soft strings for the sunrise, celesta sparkle on cards.
- **Sections**: intro (no music, 0–4.0) · R1 jig, recorder + lute (4.0–13.3, dead stop on the theft) · R2 bassoon variant (16.7–24.0, stops into the stare) · R3 pizzicato creeping (29.3–34.7) → chase, full band ×1.33 (34.7–38.0, stops on the splash) · reveal: one held low note (40–43) · finale: harp + strings + recorder, broad (46–55).
- Every card lands on a bar downbeat; every dialogue box on a beat; the theft, the splash and the title on downbeats. The checker (`tools/cuecheck.py`) compares every EV hit with the grid.

## 9. Type and subtitles

- Cards: **Cinzel** caps, wide tracking, gold metal ("RULE I"), with **Cormorant Garamond italic** under it ("Greet your first customer."). Placed in the upper third of the vertical frame (the diorama sits in the middle), over a dark translucent plate.
- Dialogue boxes: dark plate, thin gold border, corner ornaments, name plate (YOU / GULL / GRANDMA / FISH), Cormorant text typed over ~90 % of the voice, blinking caret, chime. Placed in the lower third, above the narration band.
- Narration subtitles: Cormorant Garamond italic ~54 px, centred low on a soft dark band; one line per clip; not shown for lines the card already prints.
- Title card at the end: Cinzel "YOU ARE A FISH SELLER", hold ≥ 4 s.
- `.srt` exported from the same cue data; delivered as `subtitles_en.srt` because the captions are burned in.

## 10. Voices (Kokoro, English)

- Narrator: British male storybook voice, deadpan, speed ~0.88.
- YOU: American male, bright, low-passed ~7 kHz ("in the world").
- GRANDMA: British female, slowed and pitched down a touch.
- FISH: "Nope." — American male, low and flat.
- GULL: no voice: a synthesized squawk.
