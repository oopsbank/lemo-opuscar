# Sand Trap: treatment (Toon on Set, ~26.5 s, 1080×1920, 24 fps, English)

**Question:** Why do golf balls have dimples?

**Answer (true, two sources):** the dimples stir up a thin layer of turbulent air that clings to the ball longer. That shrinks the wake behind it, so there is less drag and the ball flies about twice as far as a smooth one.
- Penn State, "Probing Question: How do dimples make golf balls travel farther?";
- University of Illinois Physics Van Q&A #945.

Lift from backspin is real too, but we keep to one mechanism: drag and the wake.

## 0. Format (research update, ideas/FORMULA.md §4)

- **Length:** 25–35 s. Frame 0: the ball huge, its dimples circled, the question spoken and on screen, and sandpaper already moving.
- **Fixed cast, introduced here and kept for the series:**
  - **Gus** is the confidently wrong one: the postman from Red Flag, with his face, mustache and blue shirt. Here he wears a plaid golf cap and carries tees in his pocket. Voice `am_puck`. His signature opener: **"Oh, I KNOW this one."**
  - **Nana** is the one who knows: the grandma from No Holes, with her grey bun, round glasses, plum cardigan and cream apron. Voice `bf_isabella` at 0.85–0.9. Her signature: **"Actually…"**, always on the same sting (a bright two-note marimba + glockenspiel).
- **❌ WRONG stamp** + buzzer at the end of the wrong theory.
- **Loop:** Gus, unrepentant, eyes a *tennis* ball: "Wait. Why do tennis balls have fuzz?" Up comes the sandpaper. The hard cut lands on frame 0: sandpaper on a ball in close-up. It also teases the next film in the series, Sports Balls #2: tennis-ball fuzz.
- **Comment question:** "What did YOU think dimples were for?"

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Fixing what isn't broken (chosen).** Gus "fixes" his ball by sanding the dimples off. It plops off the tee. WRONG. Nana: "Actually…", and the smoke-wake demo. A real ball rockets out of sight. Gus turns the sandpaper on a tennis ball (loop).
2. **The race.** Smooth and dimpled balls hit side by side, the camera following both. It's clear, but one joke stretched over 30 s, and it has no wrong theory to stamp.
3. **Three wrong guesses.** Grip, decoration, counting. It's funny, but the answer is only told, never shown.

**Why 1:** a wrong theory with a visible consequence, a demo only this medium can do (smoke lines around two balls in a lit set), and the loop comes for free (the fool repeats the mistake on the next ball).

**How it differs from the earlier films:**
- **Opening:** an extreme close-up insert of the object with a ring overlay. Red Flag opened on a mailbox wide; No Holes opened on the counter.
- **Structure:** WRONG stamp + "Actually…" in the middle.
- **Signature shot:** the wind-tunnel smoke board.
- **Camera:** a ground-level low angle, then a tilt up after the drive.
- **Score shape:** a jaunty bed, dead stop, buzzer, sting, then a rising run.
- **Ending:** a loop, not a payoff.

## 3. Look

- **Set:** a tee box at golden hour. Striped mown grass, a tee marker pair, a ball washer post, a bench, fairway rolling away, a bunker, trees in the far blur, a flag far away. Low sun from front-left, long shadows.
- **Ball:** real geometry with ~330 dimples; "sanding" interpolates the dimple depth to 0 and the roughness to mirror-shiny.
- **The smoke board:** a black board on an easel. The smoke is drawn into the board's canvas texture every frame: potential-flow streamlines round each circle; separation at about 85° for the smooth ball and about 120° for the dimpled one; curling eddies drifting downstream in the wake. The real 3D balls sit in front on thin rods.
