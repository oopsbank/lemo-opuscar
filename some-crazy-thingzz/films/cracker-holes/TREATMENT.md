# No Holes — treatment (Toon on Set, ~24 s, 1080×1920, 24 fps, English)

Topic: **Why do crackers have holes?** Answer (true): the holes ("docking") let steam escape while the dough bakes, so the cracker stays flat and evenly crisp instead of blistering and puffing up.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The skipped step (chosen).** A kid baking crackers decides holes are pointless, skips them, and the dough puffs into a pillow that bursts the oven open. Grandma pokes it, the steam whistles out, the narrator gives the answer, and the kid does it right the second time and gets the crunch.
2. **Three trays.** A countdown in the oven: no holes, a few holes, many holes, side by side. The three results show the answer. It's clear, but it reads like a lab demo, with no one to laugh at.
3. **Inside the dough.** Steam bubbles as tiny characters, trapped under the crust, looking for an exit until a fork opens doors for them. It's charming, but the style is eye-level and full-size: a microscopic world breaks the set.

**Why 1:** it is a demonstration with a consequence (the style's "question → demonstration → reaction"), it has one big visual gag you can read at thumbnail size (a cracker the size of a pillow), and it ends with the character doing it right, not with a callback. The opening is mid-action, not an insert.

## 2. Logline and arc

A kid who thinks cracker holes are pointless learns, with a bang, that the steam needs a way out.

- **Setup:** a counter, a dough sheet, a fork. "Holes? Who needs holes!" The fork goes over the shoulder.
- **Turn:** through the oven window the dough bubbles, then swells into a pillow. The door bangs open.
- **Ending:** Grandma's fork lets the steam out. The kid pokes a smiley face of holes, and the cracker comes out flat and golden. Crunch.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Kitchen gags in stop-motion comedy shorts** (a breakfast machine going wrong). Learn: wide, then insert, then reaction; anticipation before every mishap; one loud sound and then silence. Don't take: characters, designs, machines, music.
- **Warm animated kitchen scenes** (the steam-and-copper look of feature animation). Learn: an amber interior key, steam as the thing that catches the light, a window sunbeam. Don't take: any shot, character or set design.

## 4. Look

A warm home kitchen in the afternoon. Mint wall tiles, cream painted cabinets with worn edges, a butcher-block counter, a vintage **butter-yellow enamel oven** with chrome trim and a glass window (the hero prop), and a window with the sun coming in (a hard sun patch on the floor and the counter). Inside the oven, the orange glow of the elements. Steam and flour are soft billboards lit by the same light.

Cast (new):
- **The kid**, about 1.15 m: a tomato-red T-shirt, navy shorts, an oversized white chef's hat (toque) that slips and pops, a brown tuft of hair. A jumpy voice.
- **Grandma**, about 1.55 m: a plum cardigan with a cream apron, a grey bun, round glasses. A calm, deadpan voice.

## 7. Cue map

120 BPM, F major, 4/4, 12 bars.
- Bars 1–3 (0–6): a bouncy groove of pizzicato bass and marimba with clarinet staccato; a woodblock on 2 and 4.
- Bar 4 (6–8): a bassoon walk-up into the oven.
- Bars 4.75–5 (7.5–10.5): tension. Tremolo strings and a rising chromatic pizzicato, getting louder, with a xylophone tick for each bubble.
- 10.5: a sting (a low brass/bassoon "bwomp" with a cymbal-like noise burst). Silence until 14.8.
- Bars 8–10 (14.8–20): the groove returns, lighter (marimba + pizzicato), the pokes on eighths.
- Bars 10–12 (20–24): a happy resolution, the ding on the downbeat at 20, and a button chord at 23.5.
